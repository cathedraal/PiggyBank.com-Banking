What Are Cookies

Cookies are small text files placed on your device — computer, smartphone, or tablet — when you visit a website or use an application. They are widely used across the internet to make websites work more efficiently, remember your preferences, and provide information to website owners.

Cookies are not programs and cannot execute code. They cannot carry viruses or install malware on your device. They simply store small pieces of information that help us recognize your browser or device on future visits.

Similar technologies include:

  • Web beacons — tiny transparent images embedded in pages or emails that allow us to track whether a page or email has been opened.
  • Local storage — similar to cookies but stored directly in your browser with no expiration date unless explicitly deleted.
  • Session storage — temporary storage that is cleared automatically when you close your browser tab.
  • Pixels — code snippets embedded in pages to collect data about user behavior.
